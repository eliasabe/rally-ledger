How ratings work: everyone starts at 1000. It's a standard Elo rating (K=32) that updates after every match based on how likely each player was expected to win — beating a higher-rated opponent earns more, beating a lower-rated one earns less, and there's no floor or ceiling, so ratings move freely up or down. A tied match leaves ratings untouched. A dominant win also earns more than a narrow one: sweeping the sets (3-0) counts more than a bare 1-0, and a longer match won 2-1 counts a little more than a 1-0 too — up to 60% more swing for the most lopsided results.

Confirmations: a newly logged match sits as pending and has no effect on anyone's rating until the player who did not log it confirms it — never the person who recorded the result. Disputing a match rejects it permanently; it stays visible in history, struck through, but never counts.

Joining: anyone with the link can request to join, but a new player only appears on the ladder once the admin approves the request — enforced by the page itself, not just good manners.

Identity: the page recognizes you automatically, tied to the specific browser you joined from — there's nothing to log into and nothing to pick from a list. That's also why it's one device per player: joining from a different browser or device later won't be recognized as the same person.
